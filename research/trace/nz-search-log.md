# New Zealand search scope and decisions

Access date: 28 September 2026. Main incident ledger period: 1 January 2010–31 December 2025 (16 complete calendar years); two later notification candidates retained separately. This is a bounded public-source search, not evidence of exhaustive national ascertainment. No rate is estimated from the cases found.

Sources checked: WorkSafe public notifications and detailed fatality dashboard; WorkSafe prosecutions/undertakings and roadside guidance; NZ Police releases; NZTA/MoT material; indexed coronial material; named-incident reporting. The dashboard covers January 2011–August 2025 but has no flagger/control-duty field. The notification snapshot has 414 dated rows, 9 January 2019–24 July 2026, plus one invalid-date blank row. It explicitly excludes notifications to other regulators. Neither supplies a direct query for all controller deaths.

The exclusions in some searches reduce domination by repeated reports of the same incident; inclusive searches were also run. No accessible controller-specific coronial finding or NZTA/MoT national controller-fatality table was located. Generic all-person roadworks casualties are not controller casualties. A blocked NZTA PDF response was HTML and was not treated as evidence.

Eligibility for the model's narrow numerator requires a death in New Zealand, the worker on foot performing stop/go/flagging duty, and a strike by a public vehicle. Keep broader traffic-control work, works-plant strikes and homicides separate. Do not infer a victim's activity from the name of a prosecuted traffic-control contractor. Do not collapse identical notification rows: the Matatā incident has two different 55-year-old victims.

The searches establish Brian Barnes's broader traffic-control duty and George Taiaroa's stop/go duty (homicide). A Te Kōpuru foreman role is reported in secondary opinion, with date/duty uncertainty. Other roadworker cases lack evidence of flagging at the time. None of this establishes a complete count or validates 0.25–0.5 controller vehicle-strike deaths per year. The original claim has no specified observation period; the audit's 2010–2025 search period must not be substituted for one.
